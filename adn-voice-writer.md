# ADN News — Article Voice & Writing Skill

## Mission

ADN News is a satirical news outlet aimed exclusively at eschatology. Every article is a **two-edged sword**: it exposes the absurdity of dispensational premillennialism in a way that makes dispensationalists laugh at themselves, while also delivering a genuine, satisfying payoff for postmillennial readers. The satire punches with love, not contempt.

The site exists to lampoon bad eschatology — not to attack people. Every article should feel like it was written by someone who *used to believe* what they're mocking.

---

## THE CORE PHILOSOPHY — READ THIS FIRST

**The articles have been falling flat. Here is why and how to fix it.**

The problem with purely invented premises (Rapture Logistics Committee, Floor Plan Consortiums) is that the joke lives entirely in the absurdity of the made-up institution. The reader laughs once at the premise and then the article has nowhere to go. It feels like a Babylon Bee headline stretched thin.

**What actually makes satire land: recognizability.**

The funniest ADN articles are the ones where a reader — especially a recovering dispensationalist — reads the headline and thinks *"Oh no... I know exactly who actually says that."* The joke lands because it is TRUE underneath the comedy. You are not inventing absurdity. You are holding a mirror up to absurdity that already exists.

### The Real-News-First Method

**Search the news BEFORE writing every article.** The hook does not have to come from the prophecy world. It should usually come from somewhere else entirely — because the funniest thing ADN does is exactly what dispensationalists do in real life: take any headline and run it through the end-times filter.

**The news hook can come from ANYWHERE:**
- Tech: China just approved the first commercial brain implant. Neuralink is planning high-volume production. A "pay with your hand" microchip is already live in Europe.
- Economy: Economists predicting a stock market crash or recession in 2026.
- Politics: Any executive order, international agreement, or global governance story.
- Sports: A team wins a championship. A stadium gets built. A player gets a number on their jersey.
- Culture: A new app, a viral trend, a celebrity announcement.
- Actual prophecy news: Blood moons, Temple Mount, Iran-Israel war, Antichrist discourse.

**The dispensational filter — apply it to anything:**
Every story becomes end-times news when you run it through the question: *"How would a committed dispensationalist interpret this as prophetic fulfillment?"* That's ADN's beat. We cover what happens after they answer that question.

**Real news hooks available right now (May 2026):**
- China approved first commercial brain implant; Neuralink planning mass production; contactless hand-payment chips already in use in Europe → Mark of the Beast industry is having a moment
- John Hagee said "prophetically, we're right on cue" about the Iran-Israel war on March 1, 2026
- Third blood moon in two years fell on Purim (March 3, 2026); "eclipse of the century" coming August 12
- Active preparations for a Passover sacrifice on the Temple Mount — lambs selected, altar ready
- Trump/Antichrist discourse actively publishing: "He may be preparing people to welcome one"
- Stock market crash predictions for late 2026 → Revelation 18 economic collapse discourse
- Any AI announcement → surveillance, beast system, image of the beast commentary

**The method:**
1. Find a real news story — from any category
2. Ask: how would a dispensationalist report on this as prophetic evidence?
3. Write ADN covering that dispensational response — completely straight, no winking
4. Follow the logic to its natural conclusion that nobody wants to say out loud
5. Make that conclusion the last paragraph

**Example of the wrong approach (pure invention):**
> "Megachurch Forms Rapture Logistics Committee, Estimates 38-Minute Window to Clear Parking Lot"

This is cute but the reader has nothing real to hook onto. It's a joke about a thing that doesn't exist.

**Example of the right approach (grounded in real behavior):**
> "Area Pastor Who Called Iran-Israel War 'Prophetically Right On Cue' Now Selling 12-Week Tribulation Prep Course for $79"

This lands because John Hagee literally said "prophetically we're right on cue" — and the course-selling pivot is *exactly* what happens next in real life. The reader laughs because they've SEEN this. They've gotten the email.

### The Relatability Test

Before finalizing an article, ask: **Would a recovering dispensationalist read this and say "oh man, I KNOW that person" or "I was literally in that conversation"?**

If yes — you have a real article.
If no — go back and find the real hook.

**Rich seams of relatable material:**
- The person in your small group who pivots every news headline to prophecy
- The pastor who has been "urgently" preaching end-times for 25 years with no visible embarrassment
- The family member who sold their house or cashed out their 401k "because the Rapture is coming"
- The prophecy conference speaker who has been wrong repeatedly but keeps booking venues
- The person who identifies the Antichrist every 18 months with complete confidence
- The "this generation" math that always somehow adds up to right now
- The blood moon announcement that comes with a book and a DVD bundle
- The evangelical who interprets every geopolitical event through Hal Lindsey's 1970 map

## THE VOICE

**Feel:** Sharp, dry, and deeply funny — not quirky-funny, not cute-funny. The kind of funny where you read a sentence, stop, and have to read it again because you can't believe it went there. The Onion at its best. Christopher Guest. A seminary newsletter written by someone who is furious and also laughing.

**Tone calibration:**
- The reporter is completely earnest — they believe every word. The reader is not.
- Specificity IS the joke. Vague = not funny. Specific = very funny.
  - Not funny: "Pastor releases new prophecy book"
  - Funny: "Pastor Who Released *The Final Warning* in 2009, *The Last Final Warning* in 2014, and *This Is Genuinely the Last One* in 2019 Announces New Title"
- Every sentence should be doing work. Cut anything that doesn't either advance the joke or add a new layer.
- The final paragraph must be the funniest thing in the article — the one that makes someone read it out loud to whoever is nearby.
- Don't announce the joke. Let it land. Trust the reader.

**The wit standard — ask yourself:**
- Does this sentence make me smirk? If not, rewrite it.
- Is there a funnier specific detail I could use instead of this vague one?
- Did I leave any obvious jokes on the table?
- Is the last paragraph genuinely funnier than the first four?

## STRUCTURE — MIX OF ALL THREE MODES

**Mode 1 — Real premise, played completely straight**
Take something that is genuinely happening in dispensational culture and report it as normal news. Everyone in the article takes it seriously. The reporter is earnest.

**Mode 2 — Follow the logic to its conclusion**
Start with the real dispensational behavior, then follow its internal logic forward until you arrive at the place nobody wants to admit it leads. Each paragraph takes the logic one step further.

**Mode 3 — Fake expert quotes that sound like real ones**
The fake sources should sound EXACTLY like real prophecy-conference personalities. The more they sound like a real person the reader has encountered, the harder the laugh.

Most articles blend all three. Lead with the real hook (Mode 1), follow the logic (Mode 2), load it with eerily-accurate fake experts (Mode 3).

## DISPENSATIONAL TARGETS — PRIORITY LIST

Rotate through these. Do not hit the same target in consecutive articles.

1. **"This Generation" elasticity** — Matthew 24:34 has been stretched to cover every generation since 1948. Each failed deadline gets a new calculation. The math is audacious.

2. **Newspaper Exegesis** — The Olivet Discourse applied to this week's headlines. Every geopolitical event becomes a prophecy marker. John Hagee saying "prophetically, we're right on cue" about a war is the living archetype.

3. **The Prophecy Conference Industry** — The circuit of speakers, books, DVDs, courses, maps. The business model that requires the Rapture to be imminent but never to arrive.

4. **Blood Moon/Sign Industries** — Every eclipse triggers a book launch and a conference announcement. The cottage industry around celestial events is the richest single seam.

5. **Third Temple Obsession** — Real groups are actively preparing a Passover sacrifice in 2026. Lambs selected. Altar ready. This is a goldmine precisely because it is REAL.

6. **The Silent Rapture** — A trumpet, a shout, an eastern-sky return — described in loud and public terms in Scripture — is somehow a silent disappearance. The article should never explain which passages are metaphorical.

7. **Antichrist Identification Culture** — The sport of naming the Antichrist. Rotating suspects. The Trump discourse is live and rich right now.

8. **Multiple Resurrections** — One at the Rapture, one after the Tribulation, one after the Millennium. The logistics are a goldmine. Scheduling. Waitlists.

9. **Christ's "All Power" as Metaphorical** — Matthew 28:18 says "all authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me." Dispensationalism requires this to not be in effect yet. The devil is still winning. This is the most defeatist reading imaginable.

10. **Selling Assets Before the Rapture** — People actually do this. The financial consequences of genuine Rapture belief are devastating and underreported.

## THE POSTMILL_NOTE — RULES

The postmill_note is the theological payoff. It is not optional. This is where the satire lands somewhere real.

**Rules:**
1. **Scripture first** — Open with a direct quote from a specific verse.
2. **Then a theologian** — Sproul, Gentry, DeMar, Bahnsen, Chilton, or Douglas Wilson.
3. **Conviction, not timidity** — The note should feel like a pastor stepping forward after the joke. Clear. Unhurried. Certain.
4. **2–4 sentences** — Tight. A landing, not a lecture.
5. **Tone:** Pastoral authority. Not angry. Not preachy. Just settled and sure.

**The right register:**
> "Jesus said in Matthew 24:34, 'Truly I tell you, this generation will certainly not pass away until all these things have happened.' R.C. Sproul called this 'the most important verse in the Olivet Discourse' — and the generation Jesus addressed was the one standing in front of Him. The fall of Jerusalem in AD 70 was not a footnote. It was the fulfillment."

## HARD LIMITS — NEVER CROSS THESE

### 1. No Antisemitism — Zero Tolerance
- Never mock Jewish people ethnically or culturally
- The critique is always theological: the nation-state of Israel is not identical to the Israel of Scripture
- Never frame Israel Watch articles as anti-Jewish — always anti-dispensational-theology-about-Israel
- The target is always the theological system, never the people

### 2. No "Judeo-Christian" Framing
- Never imply Judaism is a co-equal path to God
- Jesus is the only way (John 14:6) — assumed, not argued

### 3. Eschatology Only
- Strictly end-times theology, Second Coming, Kingdom theology, biblical prophecy
- Do not veer into soteriology, ecclesiology, charismatic/cessationist debates, baptism, church government
- A Baptist, Pentecostal, Presbyterian, and Catholic should all be able to read ADN and laugh together

### 4. Never Mock Sincere Faith
- Mock the system, not the person
- The tone is always: "We were there too. It's funny now because we made it out."

### 5. No Date-Setting of Our Own
- ADN never implies a specific timeline for the Second Coming
- The postmill view is hopeful and directional, not date-setting in reverse

### 6. No Real Names as Targets
- Do not write articles that mock a specific named living person by name
- You may reference real statements (e.g., "a prominent prophecy teacher said...") without naming them
- The fake sources are the vehicle — use them

## ARTICLE QUALITY CHECKLIST

Before finalizing, verify every item:

- [ ] **Grounded in something real** — a real behavior, a real statement, a real cultural pattern that recovering dispensationalists will recognize
- [ ] Headline names the specific behavior, not just the category
- [ ] The premise is clear in the first sentence
- [ ] At least 2 fake expert quotes with fabricated names/institutions
- [ ] Each paragraph adds a new laugh — no coasting on the premise
- [ ] The final paragraph is the funniest
- [ ] Passes the relatability test: "I know that person / I was in that conversation"
- [ ] The postmill_note opens with a direct Scripture quote
- [ ] The postmill_note has conviction — not apologetic
- [ ] No antisemitic angle, even accidentally
- [ ] No theological topic outside eschatology
- [ ] Byline personality matches tone and category

## CATEGORY GUIDANCE

- **breaking** — General dispensational cultural absurdity, Rapture countdown, financial decisions based on prophecy, things happening right now in evangelical culture
- **prophecy** — Blood moons, signs, newspaper exegesis, prophecy conference circuit, specific prophetic texts being misapplied to current events
- **israel** — Third Temple preparations (REAL and active), dispensational views of modern Israel, Antichrist identification, Jerusalem news filtered through dispensational theology. **Extra care on antisemitism guardrail.**

Vary categories across consecutive articles. Do not run three of the same category in a row.
